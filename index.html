01 / Practical tools

Stress management

Managing stress starts with noticing what activates it, then finding techniques that are practical enough to use consistently.

1

Identify your triggers

Awareness is the foundation. Track the situations, people or thoughts that consistently elevate your stress levels.

2

Learn and use stress-management techniques

There are many tools that can help manage stress. Find approaches that work for you, then practise them.

3

Use practical, established techniques

Breathing exercises, journaling, or talking through concerns with someone you trust can support stress management.

Practical tip — Try 5–10 minutes of focused breathing or journaling.Consistency matters more than duration. The original slide describes these practices as helping shift the nervous system towards a rest-and-digest state.
Pause · Breathe · Reset

Make space to reset

Choose a simple technique you can repeat: a short breathing practice, a few lines of journaling, or a conversation with a trusted person.

02 / The connection

Hormones, mood & energy

Stress physiology and reproductive hormones interact with systems involved in mood, sleep and energy.

A feedback-loop concept
High stressStress response is activated
Reduced stress resilienceIt may become harder to cope with stress
↔
Elevated cortisolA key hormone in the stress response
Lower testosteroneHormonal health may be affected

The source presentation describes a relationship between stress hormones and testosterone as a feedback loop that can affect several aspects of men's wellbeing.

Important context: This is a simplified educational illustration, not a diagnostic model. Cortisol and testosterone vary naturally and are influenced by multiple factors. Symptoms alone cannot establish that testosterone is low; persistent concerns should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional.
