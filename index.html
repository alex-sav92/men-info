1 / Mental wellbeing

Mental Health

1.1 / Recognize the signs

Signs & symptoms of poor mental health

Persistent worrying

Worry that feels difficult to control.

Overwhelming sadness

Persistent or overwhelming feelings of sadness.

Social withdrawal

Pulling away from friends and family.

Difficulty concentrating

Finding it harder to focus.

Tiredness or fatigue

Feeling persistently tired or low on energy.

Physical symptoms

For example, shortness of breath or headaches.

⚠ More common patterns in men

Substance abuse

Using substances as a way of coping.

Anger & irritability

Distress expressed through frustration or a shorter temper.

Working obsessively

Staying constantly busy or over-investing in work.

Sleep problems

Difficulty sleeping or disrupted rest.

Reckless behaviour

Taking risks or acting impulsively.

These are possible signs, not a diagnostic checklist. Experiences differ between individuals, and no single behaviour proves that someone has a mental health condition.
1.2 / Why speaking up matters

The Cost of Silence

When concerns go unexpressed and symptoms are ignored, the effects can build over time and touch many areas of health.

Stress response

Chronic stress response

Prolonged stress can keep the body's stress-response systems activated. Chronic stress is associated with effects on immune function and overall health.

Delayed attention

Ignored symptoms

Physical symptoms may be dismissed or minimised until they become more serious and require more intensive attention.

Disconnection

Emotional isolation

Unspoken pressure can create isolation, disconnecting men from potential support and compounding mental health challenges.

The good news: These patterns can change. Early support and sustained care can improve wellbeing; it is worthwhile to seek help at any stage.
2 / Midlife and hormonal health

Andropause

2.1 / Midlife hormonal health

What is Andropause?

Andropause is a commonly used term for age-associated changes in men's hormones and wellbeing. Unlike menopause, these changes are generally gradual and vary considerably between individuals.

A gradual process

Not a single moment in time

The supplied material describes testosterone as declining by around 1–2% per year after age 30 and frames the process as unfolding over decades rather than months. The rate is not universal; individual levels and trajectories vary.

30+

Possible changes

Physical & mental effects

Physical: muscle loss, increased body fat and reduced energy.

Mental: mood shifts, cognitive changes and irritability.

These changes have many possible causes and do not, by themselves, establish low testosterone or an andropause diagnosis.

2.4 / Beyond testosterone

Hormones & neurotransmitter changes

Hormonal signals interact with the brain, metabolism, sleep and stress-response systems. Wellbeing is influenced by more than one hormone.

Human growth hormone (HGH)

Falling HGH levels with age may affect tissue maintenance, sleep-related physiology and body composition.

Leptin

Leptin helps regulate appetite and energy balance. Changes in leptin signalling may influence hunger and weight regulation; the relationship is more complex than a simple decrease.

Insulin resistance

Reduced sensitivity to insulin can make blood-glucose regulation harder and is associated with increased metabolic health risks.

Brain plasticity

Changes in neural adaptability are studied in relation to ageing, mood and cognitive function. They do not alone explain depression or cognitive changes.

4 / Everyday foundations

Toolkit

4.1 / Foundations for wellbeing

The Men's Health Toolkit

Four practical pillars can support sustainable wellbeing. The key is consistency, realistic goals and choosing habits that fit everyday life.

↗
Movement

Exercise

Aim for 2–3 resistance-training sessions weekly plus cardiovascular activity. Strength training supports muscle and physical function; cardio supports mood and cardiovascular health. Choose activities you enjoy so they are easier to sustain.

◉
Fuel

Nutrition

Eat to fuel your body and mind. Focus on whole foods, adequate protein, healthy fats and plenty of vegetables. Consistent, balanced eating can help support steady energy and mood.

☾
Recovery

Sleep

Aim for 7–9 hours nightly, with consistent sleep and wake times. Good sleep habits support recovery, emotional regulation and hormonal function. Rest is an investment, not an indulgence.

◎
Belonging

Connection

Cultivate meaningful relationships where you can be authentic. Regular connection with friends, family or support groups can provide perspective, reduce isolation and build resilience.

4.2 / A different perspective

Mindset Matters

How we think about health, strength and support can shape our willingness to make changes and ask for help.

01

Vulnerability is strength

Being honest about struggles, seeking help when needed and showing up authentically takes courage. Resilience includes recognising when support is needed.

02

Celebrate small wins

Sustainable change is built through accumulated small steps, not only dramatic transformations. Acknowledging progress can reinforce helpful behaviours and build momentum.

03

Practise gratitude

A regular gratitude practice — such as noting three things each day — can help shift attention towards positive experiences and support a more balanced perspective.
